# Helping Your Child Grow Up Smart with AI

**Your child learned to be an AI Detective today. Here's how to keep the learning going at home.**

## Voice scams: AI can copy voices

Scammers can copy a loved one's voice from a short video or voice message. They call pretending to be family in trouble and ask for money fast.

- **Create a family password** together, a silly word only your family knows.
- **Hang up and call back** on a number you already know.
- **Slow down.** Pressure and urgency are warning signs.

## Games and chats

Strangers can reach children through game chats, apps, and messages.

| Watch for | What to do |
|---|---|
| "Free Robux or V-Bucks, just give me your password" | Teach: free stuff that asks for a password is always a trap |
| Someone asking for photos, address, or school | Keep personal information private |
| "Don't tell your parents" | This is a red flag. Tell your child they will never be in trouble for telling you |
| Mean messages or fake pictures of classmates | Don't reply, save it, report it to the school |

## AI and homework

AI can be wrong, and it can't do the learning for your child.

- Ask: **"Did you think about this yourself first?"**
- Use AI **together**, as a helper for questions, never to do the work.
- Remember that many AI chatbots are made for ages 13 and up.

## Talk about it at dinner

Try these questions with your child:

1. "Show me a video. Real or AI? What's your clue?"
2. "What would you do if someone online asked for a secret?"
3. "Who made this, and why did they make it?"

## The family rule

If something online asks for a **secret**, **money**, or a **password**:

**STOP. THINK. CHECK. ASK.**
